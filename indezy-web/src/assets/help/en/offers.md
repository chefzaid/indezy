The **Offers** feed gathers the freelance missions posted on some twenty job boards (Free-Work, Cherry Pick, LeHibou, Collective.work and others), read for you and scored against your profile. Only freelance missions get in: permanent and fixed-term jobs are left out.

## Read the feed

Each card shows the site, title, publisher, daily rate, place with its distance from your city and the estimated trip by car and by transit, work mode, duration, start, skills and the offer's age. Offers you have not seen yet are marked **New**.

![The offer feed with its filters, the profile match score and the rate versus market on each card](/assets/screens/offers-en-light.jpg)

- **Show details** unfolds the description on the card.
- A mission posted on several boards shows once, with **Also on** links.
- The **All**, **Saved**, **Applied** and **Hidden** views sort out what you have already handled.

## Filter and sort

**Describe my search (AI)** sets the filters from what you look for, said in your own words ("Java and Kafka, 650 € a day at least, 2 remote days, Île-de-France"); then save it as a search.

1. Open **Filters**.
2. Combine **Keywords**, **Leave out offers mentioning** (a sector or a client you do not want), **Daily rate (excl. VAT)**, **Regions**, **Departments**, **Distance from my city**, **Remote work**, duration, **Starting by**, **Seniority**, **Skills**, **Published within** and **Sites**.
3. Tick **Only offers I have not seen**, **Matches my profile** or **Hide suspicious offers** if you want.
4. Sort by **Newest**, **Highest daily rate**, **Closest** or **Best match**.

Filters stay in the page's address, so you can bookmark a view. An offer that states no duration, start or seniority stays listed when you filter on them.

## Profile match

Once your profile names skills, every offer gets a score out of 100: skills (60 points), rate against your target daily rate (25), place (15) and start date against your availability (10). The badge is green from 60 and amber from 35; hover it to see why. **Matches my profile** keeps offers scoring 60 or more. In your profile, **What counts in the match score** makes a part count double, or not at all (the skills always count).

> **Tip:** a profile without a city, target rate or availability date is scored on the other parts. Complete it from [Getting started](/help/getting-started) for fairer scores.

## Deal-breakers

Under an offer's title, in red, Indezy flags what can rule it out, read from the offer and your profile without AI: **On site every day**, **Rate under your target** (10 % or more below), **Beyond your commute**, **Starts before you are free**, **Portage required** and **Permanent job** (a CDI offered rather than a mission). Set your target rate, your city with your longest commute and your availability in the profile for the first ones to show.

## Rate versus market

A card that states a rate compares it with the median rate of the open offers naming the same first skill, in its region or else in France (for example "+12% vs. Java in Île-de-France"). Medians are worked out every night.

The **Market** button opens a **Market snapshot** of what your filters find: offer count, **Median daily rate**, **Middle half of the rates**, the share paying your target rate, remote work, **Most named skills**, **Most active intermediaries** and publications over the last 30 days. It ends with the **Trends** of one of your skills over 30 days, 90 days and a year. The panel links the **Quarterly market report**, open to anyone: the rates the feed's missions stated each quarter, by skill, seniority and region. Then **Skills in demand you lack**: among the open offers naming one of your skills, the share also asking for a skill your profile does not list, with **Plan a training**. **CPF trainings** on a skill lists CPF-eligible trainings from the Mon Compte Formation catalogue, those given remotely first; **Plan it** adds one to your trainings.

## Act on an offer

- **Save the offer** keeps it in the **Saved** view; **Hide the offer** takes it out of the feed.
- **Add a note** (under **More actions**) keeps up to 2,000 characters, found by the keyword filter and copied to the opportunity.
- **I applied** records how (**The site's form**, **Email** or **Phone**) and when. The mission joins the pipeline with an application step and, if you tick **Plan a follow-up**, a follow-up in your [calendar](/help/calendar). **Undo the application** forgets it.
- When you keep hiding a publisher's offers (five in 30 days), the feed offers to hide all of them; **Not now** stops asking on this device.
- When many of the offers you hid in 30 days mention a sector word (five or more, a quarter of them, none of the offers you saved or applied to), such as “banque” or “defense”, the feed offers to leave them out: **Leave them out** adds the word to **Leave out offers mentioning**. Save the search to keep it; **Not now** stops asking on this device.
- **Hide every offer from …** blocks a publisher, however its name is spelled. Manage the list in **Profile → Offer feed**.
- Checkboxes act on several offers at once: **Save**, **Hide**, **Mark seen**, **Add to pipeline**.
- On an offer's page, **Should I apply? (AI)** says in one line whether the offer is worth applying to for your profile, and lists its deal-breakers (on site every day, a rate under your target, a permanent job posted as freelance...). For an offer an ESN or a platform posted, it also says which end client it is likely for, named or described from the offer's clues. The answer is kept: **Ask again** after changing your profile.
- Turn on **Triage the new offers overnight (autopilot)** in Profile → Preferences → AI, and every night the new offers that fit your profile get that answer, 10 at most, from your AI allowance. Your dashboard lists them in the morning under **Prepared for you**, those worth applying to first, with the application prepared for the first 3 of them (email, screening answers, rate to propose) and a follow-up drafted for up to 5 contacts who have not answered you about an opportunity, each to copy into your email or dismiss.

## Add an offer to the pipeline

1. Click **Add to pipeline** on the card.
2. Indezy creates an opportunity at the Contact stage, at the offer's highest rate (else your target rate), with the publisher as client (created as an ESN if missing) and the board as source.
3. The card then shows **In the pipeline**. An offer is added only once.

Then follow the opportunity in the [pipeline](/help/pipeline).

## Saved searches

1. Set your filters, then click **Save this search**.
2. Name it and click **Save**.
3. Each search shows how many offers are new since you last opened it. The **Offers** menu item adds them up.

You can keep up to 20 searches. The bell beside a search sets its emails:

1. Click the bell (**Emails for "…"**).
2. Choose **As they come (grouped every 30 minutes)**, **Once a day** at the hour you pick (Paris time), or **Never**.

Each email lists up to 20 offers. If you open no offer from them for 60 days, the emails stop and say so. Their **Stop these emails** link works without signing in.

## Suggested searches

**Suggested searches** offers up to five searches drawn from your profile and pipeline, at your target rate or more: your job title near home, your first skills, full remote, hybrid in your region, or skills your recent opportunities asked for that your profile lacks.

- **Preview** shows the suggestion's offers.
- **Save** keeps it as a saved search.
- **Dismiss the suggestion** removes it for good: it is never suggested again.

The panel opens by itself while you have no saved search, and names the profile fields that would bring more suggestions.

## Offer page and publisher page

**Open its page** (under **More actions**) shows the offer with its full description, copies, match and **History**: rate raised or lowered, start moved, how many times it was republished in 60 days, a sign of a mission hard to fill. **Copy the link** shares it with another Indezy account.

The publisher's name on a card opens what that publisher puts in the feed: its open missions, their median rate and age, how much it republishes, its sites, and whether it is among your clients. **Hide its offers** blocks it.

## Prepare my application (AI)

On an offer's page, **Prepare my application (AI)** writes the message for an ESN email, a Malt proposal or a LinkedIn message (300 characters), your answers to the usual screening questions and a rate to propose with its reasons. Edit and copy the message, then **Add to pipeline with this pack** to keep it in the new opportunity's journal, where **Tailored dossier (AI)** downloads the dossier for it.

## Publishers you know

**Worked with** marks an offer from a company you won a mission with, as the client or the ESN. **Non-solicitation** marks one from the end client of a mission you did through an ESN whose non-solicitation clause still runs: hover it to see the ESN and until when taking a mission there without it breaches the clause. **Never answers you** marks an offer from a company whose contacts never answered the three messages or more you recorded sending them. **Likely for …** names one of your end clients when the offer mentions it: the mission is likely theirs.

## Suspicious offers

The **Looks suspicious** badge flags, with the reason, an offer with no publisher and no description, a rate far from its skill's market, the same text posted by four publishers or more, a publisher known for fake listings, or a mission reposted again and again without ever changing (six times or more in 60 days), often kept up to gather profiles. Tick **Hide suspicious offers** to stop seeing them.

## From an offer link

For an offer you found elsewhere, open **Opportunities** or the Kanban board and click **From an offer link**.

1. Paste the address into **Offer link**.
2. Click **Fill in**.
3. Check each field, then create the opportunity.

An offer from the feed fills the form as **Add to pipeline** would; **Unlink** keeps the filled form without tying it to the offer. A page on another site is read for its title and summary. An offer already in the pipeline links to its opportunity, and a salaried or expired offer is refused with the reason. Each workspace reads at most 20 links an hour.

## The browser extension

The **Indezy: add this offer** extension, for Chrome and Chromium browsers, sends the page open in your browser to Indezy. Indezy then opens the new opportunity form, filled in from that offer.

1. In the extension's options, check **Your Indezy address**.
2. On an offer's page, click the **Add this offer to Indezy** button, or right-click a link and choose **Add this link to Indezy**.
3. Check the filled-in form, then save.

**Fill an application form**: create a token in **Profile → Security → API tokens** with nothing ticked under **It reads**, paste it in the extension's options, then on a job board's application form right-click and choose **Fill this form with my Indezy profile**. It fills the empty name, email, phone, city, links, title, daily rate and availability fields, and attaches your CV (the one kept in your profile, else your dossier de compétences) to the field asking for one; never a password, another file, a cover letter or a field you already typed in, and never sends the form: check it and send it yourself. Indezy's new opportunity form for the posting then opens beside it, to keep the application in your pipeline.

The extension uses your browser's Indezy session, so sign in first. It holds no password; the token, only if you add one, stays on that computer and only reads. It reads nothing on the pages you visit unless you ask it to fill a form. It is not published in the Chrome Web Store yet.

> **Tip:** the **Job boards** page, linked from the feed, shows the state of each board read, lets you leave one out with **Show in my feed**, and lists the **Sites to visit yourself**. The feed also works from the keyboard: see [Keyboard shortcuts](/help/shortcuts).
